CASE STUDY 01

Designing a fulfillment tool around real warehouse workflows

PickPerfect was an internal Shopify application designed to make complex, highly configurable keyboard orders easier for warehouse employees to fulfill. I researched the existing process, shaped the workflows and interface, and built the application with feedback from the people using it.

MY ROLEResearch, interaction design & engineering
RESEARCH5 employee interviews + workflow observation
OUTCOME~30% reduction in packing time

01 / THE CHALLENGE

Complex products created a complicated picking process

Keyboard orders could contain many combinations of styles, colors, plates, and dependent components. Some parts required other parts to be included, so employees had to interpret complex configurations while assembling orders. The existing setup process was manual and difficult to scale across product launches.

DESIGN QUESTION How might we translate complex product configurations into clear, actionable tasks for the people fulfilling each order?

02 / DISCOVERY

Start with the people doing the work

I interviewed five fulfillment employees across roles and shifts and observed how orders moved through the warehouse. I focused on how employees interpreted pick lists, handled component dependencies, and organized work across stations. I used these conversations and observations to identify opportunities to make the process clearer and reduce unnecessary manual coordination.

01Listen

Interview employees across roles and shifts.

02Observe

Understand how orders were picked and prepared.

03Iterate

Share early versions and incorporate employee feedback.

03 / WORKFLOW DESIGN

Turn product logic into a practical fulfillment workflow

The application connected product configuration rules to the steps employees needed to complete. Rather than asking staff to interpret every dependency themselves, the tool helped organize the required components and made it easier to see where each order should go.

05 / BUILD & ITERATION

Research, design, and implementation in one feedback loop

I implemented the application using React, Redux, Shopify Polaris, JavaScript, GraphQL, and MySQL. I released early versions around product launches, gathered feedback from employees, and refined the experience as the team used it. This close connection between research and implementation helped keep the product grounded in the warehouse's day-to-day needs.

CASE STUDY 02

Making customer images discoverable for configurable products

I built an internal image platform to organize photographs of custom keyboards and make relevant images available on Shopify product pages as customers selected different configurations.

MY ROLEProduct collaboration & full-stack development
SYSTEMImage ingestion, tagging, search & delivery
SCALEApproximately 500 images

01 / THE PROBLEM

Useful product imagery was scattered across sources

Highly configurable keyboards had many possible combinations, making it difficult to provide customers with realistic visual references for every option. Relevant photographs existed across internal collections and community sources, but they were difficult to organize, search, and connect to product configurations.

02 / THE APPROACH

Create a searchable image library

Working with the CEO on the product direction, I built a full-stack system for uploading, processing, tagging, and retrieving images. A Vue.js interface supported image tagging and browsing, while the backend handled image storage and processing. The platform also supported dynamic image display on Shopify product pages based on the customer's selected configuration.

03 / IMPLEMENTATION

Build the tools behind the experience

I developed the image platform using Python, AWS Lambda, API Gateway, S3, RDS/MySQL, and Vue.js. I implemented OAuth authentication, image resizing and compression, tagging, search, and retrieval. I also improved browsing in the tagging interface through image caching.

MODE DESIGNS · LLM PROTOTYPING + CUSTOMER RESEARCH

CASE STUDY 03

Prototyping an AI-powered customer research assistant

I explored how a language-model-powered interface could make open-ended customer survey feedback easier to search and synthesize.

MY ROLEPrototype development
TOOLSLlamaIndex + OpenAI API
USEInternal exploration of customer feedback

01 / THE PROBLEM

Customer feedback held useful context, but took time to review

Open-ended survey responses could reveal why customers preferred particular product options or what they wanted to see next. Finding patterns meant manually reading and organizing comments, making it harder to explore the feedback efficiently.

02 / THE PROTOTYPE

Explore feedback through natural-language questions

I built an internal prototype using LlamaIndex and the OpenAI API that let me and the CEO/marketing team ask questions over customer survey text. I checked generated responses against the underlying source comments and used the tool to explore recurring themes and customer suggestions.

ILLUSTRATIVE INTERACTION

03 / VALIDATION & USE

Keep the source feedback in view

Because generated summaries could be incomplete or inaccurate, I checked the output against the original survey responses. The prototype helped surface themes that informed discussions about board color options and a website image database feature. It remained an internal prototype rather than a production customer-facing tool.
